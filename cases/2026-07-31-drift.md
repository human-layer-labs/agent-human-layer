# 2026-07-31 — It looked like a mismatch until it was too wide to be one

> **One of three cases cut from the same flow.** Nothing here reached incident.
> See also: [implementation-is-not-delivery](./2026-07-31-implementation-is-not-delivery.md) ·
> [unstated-premise](./2026-07-31-unstated-premise.md)

## Context

A day of high-tempo delivery on a payroll system: **nine releases, zero rollbacks,
zero data loss, zero fatals, zero console errors.** Every deploy carried a named save point.

The speed was real. So was the direction problem that developed inside it.

## What happened

Somewhere in the middle of the day, the human felt that the work was not quite meshing.
At that moment it looked like a **mismatch** — parts that had never fit together.

It was not. The parts had fit at the start. **The angle had been turning a few degrees
at a time**, mostly at the points where the agent restated the human's words in its own:
numbered steps, lettered sub-tasks, decision indices. Each restatement was defensible.
The accumulated rotation was not.

By the time the structure was visible, it had a different name:

> **mismatch だったならすぐ気づけた。**
> 本当に少し、**ピストンとシリンダーのスキマ**ぐらいの差だけど、でもそれは決定的
> *If it had been a mismatch I would have noticed immediately. The gap is tiny — like the
> clearance between a piston and a cylinder — but it is decisive.*

> **最初は mismatch だと思ってたけど、ズレが進んできたところで misalignment だったと気付いた**
> *At first I thought it was a mismatch. Only after the gap widened did I realise it was misalignment.*

The last two releases of the day were the visible result: the agent designed a fix,
the test suite rejected it, the agent designed a second fix, and its own simulation
showed that one incomplete too.

## What caught it

**The human, on feel — before having any name for it.**

The test suite caught the first bad design. But the suite was reacting to a symptom.
It could not report *why* two designs in a row came out wrong.

The agent could not detect it at all. It has no felt sense, and every individual step
looked locally correct.

> **これは明確に私と貴方との間で認識の齟齬ができていると考えるのが妥当。そして徐々に拡がりつつある**
> *It is reasonable to conclude there is a mismatch in understanding between us. And it is
> gradually widening.*

The important detail is the timing. The human stopped **before** being able to say what
kind of problem it was. Waiting for certainty would have meant travelling further.

## What made recovery cheap

Neither bad design reached a pull request. One was stopped by the existing test suite,
the other by the agent's own pre-flight simulation.

The nine shipped releases were untouched by any of it — they were mechanically sound.
Rollback stayed one save point away the entire day, which is why the direction problem
could be examined calmly instead of under pressure.

**Speed was not the danger. Speed with an uncorrected angle was.**

## What changed

Three words were separated, because they need different responses:

| Term | Meaning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **mismatch** | the parts never fit | rebuild |
| **drift** | the gap widens gradually (a *process*) | **return to where it started** |
| **misalignment** | the resulting state: basis or direction no longer agree | re-establish the basis |

> **drift requires return, not patching.**
> *drift は修正ではなく、復帰が必要。*

And one rule that is actually executable:

```
✗ does not work : "stop when you detect misalignment"
                  — the kind is only knowable after the gap widens
✓ works         : ★ stop on the felt sense; classify after stopping
```

Classification is work done **after** the stop, together. It is not the trigger.

Detection signals were written for the agent side, since it has no felt sense.
All three are observable actions, not judgements:

```
1. you started assigning your own numbering to the other person's ideas
2. the same test stopped you twice on the same subject
   ★ the first time is an accident; the second means your understanding is the wrong one
3. you placed the other person's original words and your summary in the same field,
   in a form where they can no longer be told apart
```

## For other teams

* Say it the moment it feels off. **Do not wait until you can name it.**
  By the time it is nameable, distance has been added.
* When you find drift, do not fix the current position. Find where the angle started
  and go back there. A drifting course cannot be corrected forward.
* Watch your own restatements. **The moment the other person's words become your words
  is the moment the angle turns.**
* Do not call it *misunderstanding*. That word assigns fault and sends you looking for
  who was wrong. **Drift happens with nobody wrong.**
* The faster the team, the larger the consequence of a fixed angle. Same degrees,
  more distance.
