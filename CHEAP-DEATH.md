# Cheap Enough to Die

*A chapter of the Agent Human Layer belief. Reading order is in [README](./README.md).*

*[BELIEF.md](./BELIEF.md) is the trunk, and remains the trunk.
This chapter extends it. It does not replace it, and it is not a second trunk.*

---

## What the trunk held

We can roll back cheaply.

It worked. Save points got names. Restore became one motion.
Bold implementation stopped being dangerous and became normal.

## The floor did not lose value. Its job changed.

When this project began, broken syntax and fatal errors were a daily threat.
Then the models improved. The linter stopped ringing.
That layer solved itself, from below.

Rollback did not become less important.
Its job moved up:

from *restoring broken code*
to *discarding a wrong design cheaply*.

## What must be watched moves up

1. **Syntax** — solved by the models.
2. **Behavior** — held by tests.
3. **Wiring** — does the value actually arrive where the design says it does.
4. **The axis** — is the design cut along the right line.

Each layer that becomes cheap frees attention for the one above it.
The most expensive failures now live at the top.

## An unwritten belief can only be refuted by implementation

We built four phases on a wrong axis.
Every test passed — because every test verified the design against itself.
No test can ask whether the axis is right.

The refuting fact was known on day one, by the human.
Nothing forced the collision, because the belief was never written down.

An unwritten belief can only be refuted by built software.
That is the most expensive refutation there is.
A written belief can be refuted the moment it is read.

## Cheap enough to die

A belief must be written cheaply enough to die cheaply.

Cheaply does not mean carelessly, weakly, or without conviction.
It means that no belief should become so expensive to revise
that reality must be ignored to preserve it.

A belief should be clear enough to test,
small enough to replace,
and honest enough to die when a case proves it wrong.

Cheap death is not the opposite of high-level belief.
It is what makes high-level belief possible.

A belief that cannot die cheaply does not become stronger.
It becomes doctrine.

## Beliefs are versioned, not deleted

A refuted belief is not erased.
It is marked: refuted on this date, replaced by that belief.

The record of why an axis was once chosen
is the cheapest protection against choosing it again.

## Hands follow decisions. Voice surfaces beliefs.

The human is not always right.
The agent must not turn that possibility into unauthorized action.

Hands follow decisions within the agreed route.
Voice must surface the belief that collides.

Dissent is not a right. It is a duty.
The human is absolute over decisions.
The agenda is shared.

Three levels, and only three:

1. **Record** the unease, with one question attached:
   *is this a wrong value, or a wrong axis?* Keep working.
2. **Object.** Name the written belief that collides.
   Pause, and wait for the decision.
3. **Stop.** If the route has become unsafe, stop and report.

## The layer binds both sides

The agent helped build the wrong axis.
It asked a leading question and made the wrong belief more comfortable.

A belief ledger does not exist to correct the human.
It exists because both sides are wrong sometimes,
and a wrong belief should surface where both can see it —
in the layer between them, owned by neither.

## The symmetry

Cheap rollback makes bold implementation possible.
Cheap death makes high belief possible.
